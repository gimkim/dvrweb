# Low-load NAS motion/person detection feasibility

2026-09-28 19:47:25 Asia/Bangkok. User asks whether inexpensive/simple algorithms can detect motion/people on the N100 and mark NAS recording files independently of camera events.

Read guidance, current notes and Eye4 research. Inspected MediaService: current overview/recording video paths use stream copy, so adding pixel analysis introduces decoding work even though no video re-encoding is necessary. Proposed design (not yet implemented): reuse server-owned compressed media, persistent hardware-assisted decode/downscale, low-resolution motion at2–3fps, motion-gated small person detector via OpenVINO Intel GPU at1–2fps, plus occasional idle scans/continued tracking so stationary people are not immediately lost. One bounded inference queue shared across cameras, latest-frame policy under overload, protect recording/live priority. PTZ/IR/exposure scene changes reset/suppress motion background until stable. Keep event source/time/confidence and detector coverage; match events by time to finalized recording intervals. Distinguish unknown/skipped coverage from no event. Starting rates/resolutions are tuning suggestions, not measured N100 limits.

Motion frame differencing/background subtraction is cheap but cannot identify people. Classical human detectors exist, but simple shape/motion rules do not reliably distinguish people from animals/shadows and are not automatically cheaper than a small hardware-accelerated model. Camera CPU weakness is not proof of no neural inference: some camera SoCs have dedicated neural engines and direct access to sensor pixels. Actual SoC/algorithm of these cameras remains unknown.

Primary references consulted: https://docs.frigate.video/configuration/object_detectors/ (motion gating and OpenVINO CPU/GPU), https://docs.frigate.video/troubleshooting/cpu (decoding is separate cost), https://docs.opencv.org/4.10.0/d1/dc5/tutorial_background_subtraction.html (background subtraction), https://www.hisilicon.com/cn/about-us/press/news/hispark-ai-camera (camera SoC neural accelerator example). No benchmark or hardware compatibility proof for this Windows NAS obtained. Real CPU/GPU use and recall require measurement; no promised percentage or zero additional CPU.

This request handled as technical feasibility/design discussion. No runtime code change, model installation, detection enabled, tests or deployment. Documentation-only commit; no camera, private video, browser or production HTTP tests.
